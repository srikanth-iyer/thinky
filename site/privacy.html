Privacy

Short, because there is little to say.

Your notes

Your notes stay where you keep them: plain Markdown files in a folder you choose, on your own devices. thinky has no accounts and no servers, and it never sends your notes anywhere unless you use the optional AI features (below). If you sync the folder with another app (for example Google Drive), that app's own privacy rules apply to it.

When thinky uses the internet

  • The update check. At most once a day, when it starts, thinky asks GitHub for the newest release on github.com/srikanth-iyer/thinky. When you press Update now, it downloads that release from GitHub. GitHub sees these requests like any visit (your IP address, for example); thinky sends nothing about you or your notes.
  • Pictures from the web. If a note shows a picture by its web address (![](https://…)), the picture is loaded from that website when the note is on screen, as a browser would. Pictures saved in your notes folder never leave your device.
  • AI features (optional, off unless you set them up). If you choose an AI service in Settings › AI, thinky sends to that service only what one feature needs, and only when you ask: a note (Polish note), with the names of related pages and your tags so it can link them; a picture in a note (Picture to text); or one page with the notes that link to it (Ask about this page, Suggest links, Suggest aliases and a description). Nothing else from your notes is sent, and nothing is sent automatically. That service's own privacy rules apply to what it receives. With a program on your own computer (Ollama, LM Studio), nothing leaves your computer. Your key is kept by Windows or Android, not in your notes.
  • Links you click open in your browser.
  • Send feedback (Settings › Help) only opens a page on GitHub in your browser, with your words and the app's version filled in. Nothing is sent until you send it there yourself, and GitHub issues are public.

What thinky doesn't do

  • No analytics, tracking, advertising or crash reports.
  • No accounts or sign-ins.
  • The browser extension "thinky highlights" (for Edge and Chrome) talks only to thinky on the same computer. It keeps your highlights in the browser until you send them to your notes. If you turn on "Show my highlights again", it compares the address of each page you open with the list of articles in your notes, on your computer, and does nothing on any other page; addresses are not kept or sent anywhere.

This website

This site is hosted on GitHub Pages. It has no analytics, cookies or web fonts. GitHub, as the host, may keep its usual server logs.

Try it in your browser runs thinky entirely in your browser. The notes you write there are kept in your browser's own storage on your device and are never sent anywhere; clearing the site's data in your browser removes them.
